# Fundraising draft — do not publish yet

This is a planning draft, not a live fundraising offer. The product is a working prototype; the product name, campaign budget, rewards, delivery dates, refund approach, and business details are not finalized. There are no active payment links.

## Product description to verify before use

A browser-based prototype for early-stage rural-property planning across the United States. It includes a site-layout workspace, a home-layout tool, map and terrain context, and browser-local project saves. Map boundaries and measurements are planning references, not surveys or legal property data. The software does not determine zoning, permits, engineering, or project feasibility.

## Campaign platform and tax guardrails

Platform choice depends on whether the campaign is asking for voluntary support or promising a product/reward. Compare the platforms' current eligibility, fees, payout, refund, and campaign rules directly before choosing; do not promise a reward until it can be delivered and supported. GoFundMe says donations to personal fundraisers are generally personal gifts and are not guaranteed to be tax-deductible; do not market this as a charitable donation. The IRS says crowdfunding distributions may be taxable depending on the facts and circumstances. Ask a tax professional how the specific campaign should be treated.

Official references to re-check immediately before launch:

- [GoFundMe pricing and fees](https://www.gofundme.com/c/pricing) — its U.S. individual/business pricing page currently lists a 2.9% + $0.30 transaction fee per donation; verify the rate and payout conditions in the account before publishing because fees and terms can change.
- [GoFundMe tax information for donors](https://support.gofundme.com/hc/en-us/articles/360039267752-Tax-information-for-donors)
- [IRS crowdfunding guidance](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-reminds-taxpayers-of-important-tax-guidelines-involving-contributions-and-distributions-from-online-crowdfunding)
- [GoFundMe business fundraising overview](https://www.gofundme.com/c/start/business-fundraising)

## Before creating a campaign

- Resolve a commercially usable product name. Preliminary web research surfaced an existing property-management business using the exact AcreForge name and acreforge.com; this may be a conflict and requires a trademark attorney's review. Do not treat the name as cleared.
- Decide a realistic funding target from a written budget and say exactly what funds will pay for.
- Promise only features and delivery dates that can reasonably be delivered; disclose the solo-project status, uncertainty, and what happens if rewards cannot be fulfilled.
- Choose a platform and follow its current rules, fees, identity checks, refund practices, and eligibility requirements.
- For a support-only campaign, do not imply that a contribution buys a subscription, reward, ownership, or guaranteed access. Set any product/reward terms only after product scope, taxes, customer support, and refund terms are determined.
- Do not describe a donation as tax-deductible or offer an ownership/investment return unless professionally structured and legally reviewed.
- Keep payment links disabled until the real offer, business identity, privacy notice, terms, cancellation flow, and customer support contact are ready.
