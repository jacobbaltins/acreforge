# AcreForge

### Starting AcreForge

On a first launch in a new browser profile, AcreForge opens a blank conceptual project. Choose **Start a new property** to name it and optionally enter a planning idea. An idea opens in the local, rule-based planner; review it and choose **Generate plan** to create a proposal. Without an idea, AcreForge opens the editable Site Designer. Nothing is placed automatically by the welcome screen.

Choose **Explore sample project** to open AcreForge's illustrative starter layout. It is not your property, surveyed data, or real terrain. The sample and your own property are saved under separate local browser keys; use the sample banner to return to your property or reset only the sample. The sample can be reopened later from **? Start**. When your own project already contains work, **? Start** offers to continue it rather than replace it. The Projects dashboard remains available in the main navigation and links to the Site Designer and Property Planner.

Existing saved or recovery data is not cleared. Closing the welcome is remembered in this browser. Project and preference persistence require browser storage; export backups to retain work outside that browser profile. AcreForge stores one active user property plus the separate sample workspace; it does not provide multi-project cloud sync.

### Saved flat-plan views

In Site Designer, pan and zoom the flat plan, enter a name under **Saved plan views**, and choose **Save current view**. Open a saved view to return to its property-local center and zoom without changing the layout. Up to 20 views are stored with the project and can be used across its scenarios. Removal requires confirmation and cannot be undone.

Views do not capture layer visibility, the geographic map or the 3D camera. Different screen proportions can change the visible area, and a changed parcel or planning extent can change what appears around a saved local center. Backups and public exports include view names and local coordinates; review them before sharing.

### Reusable site-object presets

In Site Designer, select an existing site object and choose **Save selected object as preset** under **Reusable site objects**. Choose **Place** for a preset, then click an empty spot on the flat plan; choose **Select / move** to cancel. The new object is independent and editable, and its placement can be undone. Parcel-boundary and overlap checks use the same approximate planning rules as ordinary placement. Presets save the name, type, dimensions, shape, rotation, color and cost settings; they do not carry the original location, lock state or verification status.

Each project can store up to 50 presets, shared by its scenarios. Presets survive reload and are included in project backups **and public exports**, including their names and cost settings; review files before sharing. Removing a preset requires confirmation, cannot be undone and does not change placed objects. They are conceptual planning aids, not verified or construction-ready designs.

### Site Designer reference-image underlay

In Site Designer, choose a local PNG or JPEG under **Reference image underlay**. Images must be no larger than 5 MiB, 4,096 pixels on either side, and 12 megapixels; invalid or unsupported images are rejected without replacing the current image. Enter the image's top-left anchor X and Y and its known width and height in property-local feet, set opacity and visibility, then apply placement. Set rotation from −180 to 180 degrees; positive values turn clockwise about the fixed top-left anchor (toward the bottom of the plan). Width and height can stretch the image, so measure both dimensions carefully. Existing images without an angle open at zero degrees. This is approximate visual alignment, not survey registration or construction-ready output.

Choose **Move image on plan** to drag the visible underlay directly in an explicit move mode; the saved local X/Y anchor updates on release. The move mode temporarily prevents other plan gestures and can be exited with **Finish moving** or cancelled with **Escape**. It changes no site objects and does not establish geographic or survey alignment.

Alternatively, under **Scale from a known distance**, measure a horizontal or vertical span in the original image with an image tool, enter that span in pixels and its known length in feet, and choose **Calculate dimensions**. The calculated width and height retain the image's native aspect ratio; review its local position and select **Apply placement** to save. Pixel span must be positive and no longer than the image in the selected direction; the resulting width and height must each be 1–100,000 ft. This single-distance estimate cannot establish position, rotation, georeferencing, perspective correction or survey accuracy. Calibration is local and requires the original image to be available in the browser.

The image sits beneath editable plan geometry and does not intercept pointer input. Manually placed or traced objects remain ordinary site items and their existing 2D/3D behavior is unchanged; the image itself appears only in the 2D plan. No object detection is performed.

Image bytes remain in this browser profile's IndexedDB and are never uploaded by the import. Project storage and JSON backups contain only placement metadata, not image bytes; re-import the image after restoring on another browser or device. Public project export omits the underlay metadata. Clearing site data removes the image. Removal and replacement delete the previous local image and cannot be undone; keep your original file. Browser storage must be available.

### Property Planner driveway material allowance

Open **Property → Planner** and use **Estimate driveway material allowance** to enter a conceptual driveway length, width, material depth, and a material label. Enter your own low, expected, and high material rates per cubic yard; no market price is prefilled. The calculator converts the dimensions to cubic yards and multiplies that quantity by each rate. Inputs are saved locally with the project.

This is a material-only planning allowance, not a complete installed driveway estimate or bid. The selected material is a label and does not define a specification. The quantity does not account for compaction, waste, excavation, subgrade, grading, labor, delivery, drainage, culverts, permits, clearing, taxes, or engineering. It does not determine culvert needs or compliance. The calculator is separate from the existing overall planner budget, whose Roads & driveway line retains its generic per-foot allowance; avoid adding both without reconciling overlap. Verify quantities, current local rates, site conditions, and requirements with qualified local professionals.

### Septic occupancy and area planning proxy

Open **Property → Planner → Estimate conceptual occupancy and area** and enter all six values yourself: bedroom count, assumed occupants per bedroom, assumed daily gallons per person, assumed tank holding time, assumed liquid depth, and assumed drain-field loading. No regional, Oregon, or regulatory design defaults are supplied. The calculator estimates occupancy and daily flow, then shows a tank-volume proxy and a rectangular-equivalent tank footprint based on your depth assumption, plus a notional drain-field area based on your loading assumption. Displayed input limits are validation guardrails, not recommended values. Inputs are saved locally with the project when you calculate.

These are only arithmetic space-planning proxies, not a septic-system design, tank dimensions, site-suitability finding, or permit estimate. AcreForge does not assess Oregon permitting suitability or regulatory compliance, soil/percolation, groundwater, setbacks, well separation, replacement area, slope, or jurisdictional requirements. The preview does not draw a system on the map or add area or cost to the planner budget; reconcile it separately from the existing generic septic allowance. Confirm assumptions and site-specific requirements with the responsible local authority and qualified professionals.

### Conceptual cut, fill, export, and import volumes

In **Design → Site Designer → Terrain**, show a supported terrain grid and enter a **target elevation in relative terrain feet** under **Conceptual cut, fill & material volumes**. No target grade is prefilled. Imported elevation grids are re-centered on their valid-sample mean, so the target is relative—not an absolute surveyed elevation. Apply the target to save it with the local project; the estimate updates during terrain strokes and after undo or redo. The target remains saved when terrain is replaced; review and update it because a new grid can have a different relative zero.

The estimate samples supported building, road, parking, and arena footprints at 36 equal-area points each and treats each footprint as its axis-aligned rectangle. It sums positive terrain-minus-target differences as gross cut and negative differences as gross fill, converting cubic feet to cubic yards. Export is shown as all cut removed; fill material import is shown assuming no cut is reused. These are separate gross quantities, not a balanced haul plan. Overlapping footprints are counted independently, so totals can double-count area.

Volumes are suppressed where existing terrain guards find unknown samples, missing imported extent, invalid footprint dimensions or coordinates, rotated footprints, or footprints outside the sampled extent. The method does not model a designed surface, terrain interpolation error, parcel clipping, overlap removal, material suitability, shrink/swell, compaction, waste, topsoil, water, drainage, trucking, costs, or regulatory requirements. Plan objects are conceptual footprints; these quantities are not a grading plan, bid, or engineering estimate. Verify terrain, target elevation, material properties, and quantities with qualified local professionals.

### Local downslope routing preview

In **Design → Site Designer → Terrain**, import or create a supported terrain grid and choose **Preview downhill paths**. AcreForge routes each known grid node to its steepest lower known neighbor using the grid's local spacing; equal-height flats and local lows stop, and unknown nodes or diagonal routes across unknown gaps are not bridged. Each node contributes one relative unit. The preview reports known and unknown nodes, routed and stopped nodes, and the maximum relative contributing-node count. Teal lines show routes above a minimum node-count threshold. The preview is read-only, browser-local, and makes no network request.

This is terrain routing only—not a water-flow, rainfall, runoff, or flood simulation. It does not model rain intensity, soil infiltration, impervious cover, water volume or depth, outside-grid run-on, channels, culverts, drainage structures, mapped waters, or parcel-wide watersheds. Flat terrain and local depressions remain stops rather than being artificially filled. Results depend on the input grid's accuracy and spacing and may miss small features; edited and unverified terrain remain unverified. It is not a drainage design, engineering analysis, flood boundary, suitability finding, or permit determination. Consult qualified local professionals and authorities.

### Recent local planning improvements

- New parcel imports check coordinate ranges, duplicate consecutive vertices, zero-area rings, and self-intersections. Limits are 2,000 vertices per ring and 5,000 total. Checks do not verify hole containment, intersections between rings, or survey accuracy; previously saved boundaries remain compatible.
- The site-object list offers name/type search and type, phase, and status filters; choosing a filtered result centers it in the flat plan. Scenario snapshots can display signed deltas against a chosen baseline when both use the same supported metric version; legacy or missing metrics remain unavailable.
- Site Designer group editing uses temporary checkboxes to move, duplicate, delete, align, or distribute selected site objects. Actions reject locked selections and parcel-invalid footprints as a group and can be undone in one step. Alignment and spacing use footprint bounds; they do not check object overlap or establish engineering suitability.
- **Supplied reference inventory** in Site Designer imports a local GeoJSON FeatureCollection into a separate 3D map layer, not the proposed object list. Each feature must have `properties.kind` set to `building` or `tree`, an optional text `name`, and a 2D WGS84 `[longitude, latitude]` Point or closed Polygon with one exterior ring (no holes). Legacy CRS members, altitude, other geometries and unsupported kinds are rejected; other properties are discarded. Limits are 2 MiB, 500 features, 1,000 coordinates per ring, and 5,000 coordinates overall. The list identifies each item and its point or first polygon vertex, and can focus the terrain map; cyan represents trees and magenta buildings. References are user-supplied and unverified—not AI detection, a complete inventory, or a survey. Import is local, although opening or moving the map requests map tiles. Project backups include the inventory and source filename; public export removes it.
- The Terrain editor also imports a **local elevation grid CSV**. This is not the existing terrain-grid export format and is not LAS/LAZ, GeoTIFF, or raw LiDAR support. Choose the CSV in the Terrain editor; the file is read in the browser without a request, validated before replacement, and undoable. Existing saved projects remain compatible.
- **Cost sensitivity preview:** Under **Reports**, select overall contingency or a populated site-object type to compare −20%, current, and +20% planning estimates. The current case reconciles to conceptual CapEx; previews do not change saved assumptions, and object types without a positive available estimate are omitted. These are illustrative estimates, not bids or forecasts.

### Local elevation CSV contract

The Terrain editor's **Download re-importable CSV** exports the current grid in this same format, separate from the existing report-oriented terrain grid CSV. It works entirely in the browser and does not change the terrain. Known nodes contain relative elevations in feet using an arbitrary zero; unknown nodes are `NA`. Re-import preserves grid dimensions, extent, no-data and relative relief, but re-centers heights on the known-node mean. The file does **not** retain the original absolute elevations, datum, source, provenance, or edit history; record those separately. The filename may contain the project name. Imported grids require a recorded extent; synthetic grids use the current property dimensions. Export is refused if the generated file does not meet the import limits, including 2 MiB. This is a conceptual planning interchange, not a survey or grading design.

The first line must be exactly `row,col,x_ft,y_ft,elevation_ft`. Each subsequent line is one unquoted, comma-separated record with no surrounding whitespace; blank lines are not allowed except one final line ending. Use zero-based integer row and column indices and include every node exactly once in any order. Coordinates are **property-local feet**, origin `(0,0)` at the plan's top-left, x increasing east and y increasing south; elevation values are feet. Use uppercase `NA` for an unknown elevation, not a missing record. There is no CRS transformation or vertical-datum inference; the user must verify alignment, datum, source and accuracy.

The grid must have 4–200 rows and 4–200 columns, at most 40,000 records and a 2 MiB file limit. Each axis must be regularly spaced by at least 0.1 ft, with extent at most 100,000 ft (coordinate tolerance is 0.000001 ft plus 0.000000001 times the expected coordinate). Numeric elevations must be finite and between −2,000 and 30,000 ft, and within 300 ft of the valid-sample mean to fit the terrain editor. At least 25% of nodes and one complete 2 × 2 cell must be numeric. Values may use decimal or scientific notation. Imported heights are stored relative to the valid-sample mean; unknowns remain masked and profiles do not extrapolate. The original range and mean are retained for provenance, not as verified survey data. Edits are marked. Project backups and public exports can contain terrain and its elevation range; review them before sharing.

- A drawn utility corridor can be sampled along its approximate rectangular centerline in the terrain editor. Unknown samples stay unknown, and the result is not a surveyed route, continuous grade, or trench design.
- The Projects dashboard supports up to 50 local milestone reminders, with optional target dates, completion, editing, and confirmed removal. These are not a construction schedule.
- Reports include a phase-by-phase site-object subtotal, a contingency sensitivity preview, and an assumptions/source register. They remain conceptual estimates, not professional assessments.
- Download a compact planning-summary JSON from Reports for the active scenario; it is not a full project backup. It contains property context, building concepts, aggregate estimates, and open checklist items, so review it before sharing.

A nationwide, local-first planning workspace for rural land, farms, estates, homes, and buildings. It runs in a modern browser with no build step or paid mapping dependency. ## About and first use

The app opens on **Projects**, a map-first command center with a non-editable conceptual site-plan preview, project snapshot, and direct links to the site designer and property planner. The five primary destinations are **Projects**, **Property**, **Design**, **Reports**, and **Settings**; each destination's tabs keep its related screens available. **Property Search** accepts addresses, coordinates, full Google Maps links, and map pins, and saves up to eight recent locations in the current browser. Location search does not identify parcel ownership or boundaries; nationwide parcel lookup is not configured. Import a boundary from a county assessor/GIS or survey file in **Design → Site Designer**. U.S. state maps are available; local rules and permitting information are not built in. Use **Settings → Appearance** to change the color theme; the choice is saved in the current browser.

**Mapped roads:** In **Property → Roads near this property**, explicitly load an attributed OpenStreetMap road reference, grouped by major road, collector, local road, mapped driveway, or track/path. Labels appear only for mapped names. Category toggles affect the temporary map display only; these references are never copied into proposed site-plan roads. Coverage may be incomplete, and mapped lines do not confirm legal access or road condition. The request sends an approximate planning extent to the public Overpass API.

**Commercial status:** AcreForge is a working name, not trademark-cleared. The app is a prototype; paid plans, account sign-in, and fundraising are not active. The About page describes one explicitly unvalidated future paid-pilot hypothesis: multiple projects, cloud backup/sync, collaboration, project history, and enhanced reports. This is not a price, offer, enrollment, or evidence that users will pay; none of those features is available as paid access. Core local planning remains useful without payment. Share feedback through the user-controlled draft if you choose; no responses are sent or collected by AcreForge.

## Site designer

The conceptual 2D site designer includes a searchable library of 71 site objects across equestrian, fairgrounds, landscape, building, access/parking, and infrastructure categories. The 1,000-acre starter concept includes an access-road layout, circular round pens and turnout, feed/tack and general stores, distinct post-and-rail, board, woven-wire, electric-tape, and no-climb fences, plus native bushes, tree groups, and a grove. Add event centers, livestock halls, stages, vendor rows, camping, parking, orchards, hedges, gardens, and utilities. Work on an effectively infinite, panning canvas with a feet-based grid; use cursor-centered zoom, adjustable grid spacing and snapping, layers, and labels. Select, move, rotate, resize, duplicate, or delete objects; footprints support rectangles, rounded rectangles, ellipses, triangles, and angled polygons, and roads include straight and curved options. The isometric massing preview supports drag-to-orbit, Shift-drag or right-drag to pan, and wheel zoom; use Reset view to recenter it. Its roads follow straight and curved layouts with distinct road/path surfaces and markings; building previews now include shaded facades, windows, doors, and pitched roofs, while rotations and terrain relief are reflected in the model. Only the selected structure is labeled in the preview to reduce clutter, and secondary options are tucked into a compact menu. The template library, selected-object editor, placed-object list, and drawing-layer controls are collapsible; their open/closed state is kept while you work.

Map scale is 1 map unit = 1 foot. The plan automatically fits and centers the full property extent to the map viewport, including narrow screens, without changing scale; use Fit plan to return after panning or zooming. The 1,000-acre starter extent is a planning reference, not a limit on the infinite canvas or a property boundary. For parcel-based acreage and siting checks, import a single-boundary WGS84 KML or GeoJSON Polygon/MultiPolygon from a county assessor/GIS or survey source. The imported outline sets acreage and checks new or moved object footprints; the planning grid remains visible across the canvas. Imported parcel plans and their real-map layers are north-up; older parcel projects are migrated without changing the layout's relative positions. Acreage is calculated geodesically from the supplied outline, including holes. Property Search reports the source and total geodesic perimeter across imported polygon rings; the map outline keeps a screen-stable stroke as you zoom. Road frontage remains unavailable until mapped road geometry intersects the imported boundary, and adjacent-parcel data is not loaded. Confirm imported geometry and measurements against an authoritative survey. Paste a Google Maps or Zillow URL as a reference link; the app does not scrape those services or derive parcel lines from their pages. A reference URL is not a substitute for an authoritative boundary file.

Switch between the editable flat site plan, a U.S. state map, and a state terrain viewer. State outlines come from the U.S. Census Bureau 2024 TIGER/Line cartographic boundary dataset (1:500,000), projected in Albers equal-area, with Alaska and Hawaii shown separately. The state viewer uses MapLibre GL JS, USGS topographic or aerial imagery, and public AWS Terrain Tiles elevation data. These services and libraries do not require a paid API key; an internet connection is needed to load them.

State terrain opens in USGS aerial imagery by default; use **Topo map** to switch to USGS topographic maps. At neighborhood zoom, enable **Mapped features** to request OpenStreetMap home/building footprints and other context. Building footprints render as raised 3D blocks at city scale. OSM heights are used when available; otherwise the illustration estimates a height from floor count or a generic building type. These are visual context, not actual building dimensions.

When a selected property belongs to the chosen state, the terrain map opens centered on that location at neighborhood zoom; otherwise it opens at the state's overview. Use **Slope bands**, **Contours**, or **Hillshade** to analyze the current view from AWS Terrarium elevation tiles. The overlay status reports tile zoom, the 288-by-144-to-288 sample grid, mapped coverage, elevation range, contour interval, and slope legend. Hillshade uses northwest light; opacity and overlay visibility are saved in the browser. Analysis is bounded to 12 elevation tiles per refresh. Transparent or missing elevation is masked; if less than 70% of the sample grid is mapped or a tile request fails, the analysis overlays and 3D relief are hidden with an explicit status. This generalized, sampled analysis is for planning, not surveying, engineering, or buildability approval.

Use the terrain map's **Camera** selector for **Orbit**, **Walkthrough**, or **Fly**. Orbit supports pointer rotation/panning and map keyboard navigation; Walkthrough uses WASD to move; Fly uses W/S to move, A/D to turn, and Q/E to adjust pitch. The map keeps its standard controls and announces keyboard instructions for assistive technology. Site-plan buildings, land areas, trees, roads, and water are projected onto the selected property map and terrain from an imported parcel boundary or a location selected in that state. Proposed features use green/blue styling, separate from gray mapped references. Without a usable property reference, proposals are withheld and the map explains how to georeference them. OpenStreetMap references are optional and incomplete; estimated building heights and terrain are planning illustrations, not surveyed or verified conditions.

On **Property**, use **Load mapped buildings** to request nearby OpenStreetMap building ways for the approximate planning extent. Select a footprint to inspect its mapped polygon area, source, and available building type. Heights explicitly tagged in OSM are labeled as mapped; otherwise a floor-count or generic illustrative estimate is clearly identified. These temporary references are not saved into proposed buildings or project data, and changing the property location or parcel boundary clears them. Missing footprints do not establish that no buildings exist. Coverage may be incomplete, multipolygon relations may be omitted, and the footprints are not verified building records. © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL).

On a state terrain map, select a drawing mode to add house/building footprints, land areas, landmarks, or proposed clearing polygons. The app saves these overlays with the project and reports horizontal WGS84 perimeter, area, and polygon-side measurements. Imported parcel geometry can be displayed as a reference, but parcel lines are not fetched from Zillow or inferred from imagery. Use an assessor/GIS or survey KML/GeoJSON file for parcel geometry. Census state boundaries, imagery, and elevation are generalized planning references, not navigation, survey, or legal property data. Satellite/topographic imagery is not a complete tree or building inventory; add planning overlays manually and verify locations from authoritative sources.

The planner's **Land Data** lookup samples USGS EPQS elevation, FEMA flood zones, and USDA soil data at up to nine points inside an imported parcel (or one selected point when no parcel is imported). For multipart boundaries, it distributes samples across parcel components when possible and reports how many components received samples; hole interiors are excluded. A scanline fallback helps place samples in narrow or diagonal parcel shapes missed by the regular grid. It reports the sampled elevation range and average and coverage counts; these sparse samples are not a terrain surface, area percentage, flood determination, or substitute for survey, geotechnical, or county review. Unmapped and failed requests remain distinct from mapped low-risk results. A newer lookup cancels an older one, and results are discarded if the selected parcel or location changes while a lookup is running.

The **Property health dashboard** combines sampled terrain indicators with mapped soil-drainage samples when available. It reports in-parcel terrain coverage, leaves privacy unavailable until viewshed data exists, and labels access and utility indicators as distance-based planning estimates; those estimates are excluded from the evidence-weighted property score. Flood and soil samples remain point observations, not parcel-wide determinations. If elevation tiles are transparent or fewer than 70% of in-parcel terrain samples are valid, the analysis is not scored. Site-preparation and utility costs are illustrative planning rates, not bids or provider quotes.

The buildability map classifies in-parcel terrain cells as **candidate** (slope ≤8%), **caution** (>8–15%), **constrained** (>15%), or **unknown** when elevation or a neighboring slope sample is unavailable. These are conceptual slope-screening thresholds, not legal or engineering criteria. Cells outside the supplied parcel and inside parcel holes receive no class. FEMA observations appear only as individual sampled-point markers; sparse points are not expanded into flood boundaries or treated as proof that surrounding land is clear. Setbacks, easements, wetlands, zoning, utility service, legal access, and permitting remain unverified.

After analysis, the separate **Slope-only what-if** preview compares sampled cells against a user-entered maximum slope from 0 to 100%. It uses saved slopes rounded to 0.1 percentage point, counts unknown cells separately, excludes cells outside the sampled boundary, and does not request new data. The threshold is temporary and changes neither the main map and rankings nor the plan or budget. Counts are not buildable acreage: wetlands, easements, setbacks, flood zones, soils, access, and other constraints are not evaluated. Without an imported parcel the analysis uses an assumed square, not a legal boundary.

The **Build zones** panel ranks up to three distinct candidate locations (Zone A/B/C) from candidate or caution terrain cells. Its deterministic fit index combines slope, the terrain-derived sun-exposure proxy, terrain class, nearby opportunity fit, and mapped-road proximity when available; the score is normalized when mapped roads are unavailable. Higher-ranked terrain is not a feasibility or AI finding. Exact cells with mapped high/moderate flood sample points are not recommended, but those sparse point observations are not flood boundaries. Privacy/viewshed, legal access, setbacks, easements, wetlands, zoning, utilities, and permitting remain unverified; when no suitable cells exist, AcreForge shows no zones rather than forcing recommendations.

After property analysis, **Property opportunities** ranks suggested house, shop, pond, solar, orchard, and event locations inside candidate or caution terrain cells. Each suggestion includes a relative fit index, rationale, and missing evidence; map markers identify distinct representative cells and open the corresponding details. These deterministic terrain-screening suggestions are not feasibility findings or saved placements. Optional mapped-road proximity can inform some rankings, but does not confirm legal access; solar is not an irradiance estimate, and pond, soil, event, and other site requirements need independent verification. Missing terrain suppresses suggestions rather than being treated as favorable.

The **Property Copilot** command bar accepts questions and supported planning requests such as **“What can I build here?”**, **“Build a homestead,” “Create a compound,” “Create a venue,” “Lower costs,”** and **“Add another house.”** It uses AcreForge's local rules, not a connected generative-AI model. Property questions summarize the current analysis or start one if needed; plan and layout edits show confirmation and can be reversed with **Undo last command**. Unsupported requests are reported rather than presented as completed. Commands do not change the selected property, and analysis does not verify permits, feasibility, access, or other unmodeled constraints.

The planner's **Generate editable layout** action places requested structures and site features into the Site Designer, preserving stated house floor area and shop dimensions, spacing objects where the available extent allows, and optionally using sampled terrain context. A homestead request proposes a house, garden, orchard, coop, barn, greenhouse, and pond; optional features that do not fit are listed with reasons, while a house that cannot fit leaves the prior layout unchanged. Imported-parcel homestead placements are screened against the parcel boundary. The planner also adds conceptual well/septic assumptions and attempts a driveway stub where space and parcel constraints allow, with an explanation for each placement. Other requests that cannot fit are rejected before replacing the prior generated layout. Placements remain editable and undoable. Driveway stubs do not establish road connection or legal access; utility routes, setbacks, septic suitability, permits, and costs are unverified planning assumptions, not engineering or provider quotes.

A **family compound** request proposes a main house, parent house, guest house, and shared 40 × 60 ft shop. When all buildings fit, the planner compares closest-home spacing, straight-line walking distance, and a conceptual shared-trench estimate, then ranks utility-first, balanced, and privacy-first spacing alternatives with a relative index. Alternatives are heuristic comparisons derived from the current layout, not separately placed site plans or an engineering optimization. An undersized layout is rejected without replacing the prior generated objects; imported parcel footprints are screened against the supplied boundary. Verify actual utility routes, privacy needs, setbacks, easements, local costs, legal access, permits, and site conditions with qualified professionals.

A **ranch** request adds an editable concept program for an outdoor arena, round pen, two rotational-pasture placeholders, two turnout paddocks, feed/tack storage, hay storage, and manure storage alongside the requested house, barn, shop, well, septic, and driveway concepts. The additional site-object costs feed the same low/medium/high planning estimate. The pasture and facility footprints do not establish animal capacity, grazing or soil suitability, water supply, separation distances, animal-welfare compliance, or permits. Confirm the layout and local requirements with qualified professionals and authorities.

An **RV park** prompt such as “Plan an RV park for 16 sites, 2,500 sq ft per site” creates one editable gross-area footprint, a check-in building, restroom pavilion, and the planner's existing well/septic placeholders. Without explicit values, the concept uses 12 site placeholders and 2,000 sq ft per site; enter 1–100 sites and 250–10,000 sq ft/site to change them. These are configurable layout and cost assumptions, not standards or a provider quote. The footprint does not draw individual pads, internal circulation, hookups, dump stations, or establish occupancy, service capacity, fire access, health/permit compliance, setbacks, or septic suitability. Verify the concept and requirements with the responsible local authorities and qualified professionals.

An **event venue** request proposes a ceremony pavilion, reception hall, restroom pavilion(s), and guest parking. Parking uses 2.4 guests per car plus a 10% reserve, rounded up, with 300 sq ft per car; the generated lot is rounded to a rectangular footprint. The editable layout connects parking to ceremony and ceremony to reception with straight-line guest-flow paths when their footprints remain inside an imported parcel. Parcel-crossing routes are omitted and called out. The budget includes a 350 ft guest-circulation allowance at $10/ft until a layout is generated, then uses the generated path lengths. These are rough planning allowances, not bids. Paths do not establish accessible circulation or fire access; fire access, accessibility, occupancy, parking, zoning, and permit compliance are not assessed. Confirm event use, occupancy, parking, septic capacity, fire access, accessibility, and permits with the county and qualified design, fire, accessibility, and engineering professionals.

The Property planner's **Low / medium / high budget** itemizes roads and driveway, utility routes and well, buildings, site preparation, septic, design/permit allowances, and contingency. Default placeholders include $40/ft roads, water $28/ft, power $32/ft, fiber $14/ft, septic routing $35/ft, and shared sewer $70/ft, scaled by an editable utility factor; shared sewer distance assumes the planner distance plus 60 ft per home. Septic systems default to $32,000 (or $65,000 for venue/event concepts), with 10% site preparation, 6% design/permits, and 10% contingency. Low/high use 82%/128% of each mid line. Utility route distances can be edited per plan; named Utility Corridor objects (Water, Power, Fiber, or Septic) use their drawn layout length. Placed well/house and house/septic geometry yields a straight-line distance, never a claimed trench route; otherwise the planner-distance placeholder is labeled as an assumption, and missing distances remain unavailable. Utility sources, service availability, construction routing, permits, and septic suitability are not verified. Edit assumptions in the planner or export route and budget ranges in its CSV. Subdivision concepts use a shared sewer-main concept and mark private septic as not included. These local planning allowances are not bids, appraisals, engineering, or provider quotes; verify quantities, local rates, connection points, sewer/septic approach, and site conditions before relying on them.

In **Projects → Scenarios**, save up to 12 named comparison snapshots without switching or changing the active layout. Compare estimated capital, home-spacing privacy, property terrain buildability, generated utility-route costs, and a rule-of-thumb self-sufficiency proxy when each value is available; missing evidence stays unavailable. With two or more snapshots, compare their frozen site layouts side by side and choose which saved concept appears as “After.” The schematic previews are rendered locally from the saved layouts and current display layers; they are not terrain imagery, surveyed parcel maps, or live editable plans, and comparison never changes the active scenario. Restore creates a new editable scenario and keeps the saved snapshot and existing layouts intact. Snapshots are stored locally with the project. Estimates and scores are planning references, not bids, viewshed analysis, engineering, or feasibility determinations.

The **Mapped features** control can optionally request nearby OpenStreetMap homes, other buildings, roads, fields/orchards, woods/brush, and individually mapped trees. It sends the visible map bounds to the public Overpass API only after you enable it, and only at neighborhood zoom. Use **Reference layers** to hide categories, click a mapped feature to see its available name/category, or **Clear reference context** to remove temporary downloaded references. Clearing does not change map imagery, the saved parcel boundary, or project overlays. Reference data is temporary and is not saved with the project; coverage varies and may be missing or out of date. OpenStreetMap data is © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL); an internet connection is required. **Proposed clearing** is only a saved planning annotation; it does not alter satellite imagery or real-world vegetation.

The map can show coordinates parsed from a Google Maps link or the center of an imported parcel, along with an inset of the parcel and its site objects. Switching views does not change saved project data. New starter projects default to 200 acres (1,000 is the maximum); existing saved project acreage is preserved and can be edited in Project Settings until an imported parcel boundary sets it from the source geometry.

The **Property → AI Planner** also includes a local, approximate sun-position and seasonal day-path preview. Enter explicit WGS84 latitude/longitude or copy the saved property coordinates, then choose a local date and time. If the project has no coordinates, the fields start blank; the tool never infers a location. Clock times use the browser's configured time zone and daylight-saving rules, which may differ from the property's time zone. The chart compares the selected date with approximate June and December solstice paths and reports approximate sunrise/sunset and solar azimuth/elevation. It runs locally and does not change the saved property location. It does not model shade, trees/buildings, terrain horizon, solar-panel output, or recommend building orientation.

The designer is for early planning only: its rectangular planning extent is acreage-derived, not surveyed parcel geometry. Object dimensions and cost assumptions are illustrative and do not establish feasibility, permits, or approvals.

The isometric preview uses procedural 3D geometry for shaded buildings, circle/ellipse/triangle footprints, trees, clustered bushes, hedges, and post/rail, board, electric, and mesh fences. Select **Free rotate** and drag an object around its center, or use the object editor’s 0–359° slider/number field; no extra model or asset download is required.

Pastures and crop fields are **land zones** (Land zones card), not placeable objects: draw, move, recolor, resize, reshape, and rotate them. The **Ground surface** card sets the base biome (temperate, eastern Oregon, desert, short grass, tall grass) and lets you paint or erase local ground cover (short/tall grass, sagebrush, desert, soil); paint is saved per scenario, supports undo/redo, and can be hidden with the "Ground cover" layer. Ground colors are illustrative, not a soil or habitat survey. All code is original to AcreForge; Map3D, VoxCity and 3D City Database were reviewed only as inspiration.

## Roads, water, traffic and real-map snippet

- **Roads:** the Roads tool draws dirt, gravel, paved, street, avenue and trail segments (straight or curved, one-way optional) with per-foot build costs that feed the project budget. Roads that cross creeks or rivers get bridges, or culverts for short crossings on dirt/gravel roads; their estimated cost is included.
- **Traffic simulation:** press **Simulate traffic** to run cars and pedestrians on the network, with signal lights at street/avenue junctions, a congestion heat map, and stats for trips, stalled cars and buildings not served by a road. Simulation and movement analysis stay disabled until vehicle-access gates and connected destinations are available.
- **Concept movement analysis:** enter peak-hour car, guest, and delivery demand to compare deterministic fastest-gate routes, guest-vehicle arrivals (2.4 guests per vehicle), guest walking paths from placed parking or access gates, and a separate longest-route emergency-access probe. The report ranks modeled link loads by location, states its demand assumptions, and is not a traffic study, capacity/queue model, fire-code review, or verified emergency-access assessment.
- **Creeks & rivers:** the Water tool draws streams, creeks, rivers and canals with natural meanders. Flow is animated (disabled when your system prefers reduced motion). Ponds and lakes are placeable items.
- **Real map snippet:** **Pick location** lets you search an address, paste a Google Maps link or `lat, lon`, click the map, or use one of the preset small-town locations. **Re-capture** fetches USGS aerial imagery for your land (clipped to an imported parcel outline); it is hidden until you turn Show on. **Match real features** imports nearby OpenStreetMap roads, waterways, ponds, houses, other buildings, trees and bushes as editable items. It needs internet access, public Overpass servers can be busy (press Match again), and results are planning references, not survey data. OpenStreetMap data is © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL); imagery is from USGS The National Map.

- **Real-map synchronization:** parcels, proposed features, terrain and optional OpenStreetMap context share one WGS84-to-local-feet projection anchored on the imported parcel (or picked location) so roads, buildings and pins line up with the map. The terrain view frames a 1,000-acre context square around the 80-acre (or imported) lot; the editable plan stays at its own size. A **map source status** line reports the spatial origin, which layers (aerial/topo, terrain relief, OSM context) are available, last retrieval time, empty, stale, failed or offline states, and makes no network requests itself. Elevation no-data cells are masked rather than drawn. Dense OSM roads and outlines are simplified (1 m) for display only; measurements use source geometry, and a display cap of 4,000 features is disclosed when reached. Limits: OSM coverage is incomplete and community-edited, elevation comes from Terrarium tiles at limited resolution, and nothing here is survey data or proof that a feature does not exist.

## Data and privacy

Project data autosaves to this browser's local storage and is not cloud-synced by the app. The terrain map requests USGS imagery and AWS elevation tiles; enabling slope, contour, or hillshade analysis requests up to 12 elevation tiles for the visible terrain extent per refresh. **Import real elevation** requests tiles for the current property after you choose it. Running **Land Data** sends up to nine parcel coordinates (or the selected point) to USGS EPQS, FEMA NFHL, and USDA Soil Data Access for point lookups. The optional OpenStreetMap feature lookup sends the visible map bounds only after the user starts it. The Property road and building lookups each send an approximate planning extent to `overpass-api.de` only when their respective load buttons are selected. Public project export is intended to publish its JSON file alongside the site, so review it for sensitive information first. The [prototype data and privacy notice](data-notice.html) describes these flows and the unverified status of any server-side Stripe webhook configuration. It is not a complete commercial privacy policy.

The app can read the legacy `st3` site-planner local-storage data and migrate its site objects and financial assumptions on first launch.

### Saving, backup, and recovery

The top-bar status reports when a change is saving, saved in the current browser profile, or could not be saved. If a save problem appears, keep the page open and download a project JSON backup from **Project Settings** or **Reports**; do not assume an unsaved change survived closing the page. A separate recovery snapshot is kept in the same browser profile when a later save succeeds. Use **Project Settings → Local data & portability → Restore recovery snapshot** to review and restore it.

Use **Project Settings → Download project JSON** to make a portable backup, then **Import project JSON** to restore it. Import previews the project name and scenario/object counts and requires confirmation before replacement. It warns when a valid saved timestamp is older than the current project; malformed files and unsupported newer schema versions are rejected without replacing the current project. Cancel the confirmation to keep the current project. Import does not compare every field, so export a backup of current work before replacing it.

Project data and recovery snapshots are local to the active browser profile; the illustrative sample uses separate storage. Reference-image bytes are stored locally in IndexedDB. There is no account, server-side backup, or cloud sync. Exported JSON is downloaded to the device and leaves it only if the user chooses to share or upload it. The external map and public-data requests described above send only the selected request data when the user explicitly starts those tools.

### Interest and feedback

Open **Settings → Feedback** or the **Interest & feedback** status-bar control to answer a few basic questions about planned Pro features and product improvements. Only those selections and an optional short general note are placed in the draft; do not enter parcel addresses, coordinates, financial or contact details, or project files. AcreForge reads these answers only in the page to prepare the draft; it does not read project data, receive or save feedback, transmit it, or use analytics, and no feedback inbox is configured. **Open email draft** uses a blank-recipient `mailto:` link: your mail client may handle the draft, and you choose a recipient and press Send yourself. If no mail app opens, **Copy draft** provides a user-controlled alternative. If you send it, the content is handled by the recipient and your email provider under their policies; AcreForge does not receive it directly.

## Limits

The site map is an acreage-derived rectangular planning boundary, not a survey, GIS parcel, or legal property boundary. Terrain overlays are measured from map clicks and their accuracy is limited by map resolution and source data; they are not survey-grade. Terrain relief does not convert horizontal footprint measurements into slope-adjusted surface area. The imagery may not show every building or tree. The isometric view is a WebGL-free conceptual massing illustration. Cost, revenue, staffing, and regulatory prompts are illustrative only. No output establishes property-specific feasibility, compliance, approval, financing, or professional design.

See `CALCULATIONS.md` and the in-app Project Settings for the full disclaimer and formulas.

## Sharing with family

Each browser keeps its own copy. To share: click **Export**, send the dated .json file, and the other person clicks **Import**. Import previews project metadata and warns when the selected file has an older saved timestamp; it does not compare every field. Choose the file whose contents you intend to use, and export a backup of current work before confirming replacement. Agree on who edits at a time.

## Accounts and payments

Paid plans, account sign-in, and payment processing are currently disabled. Do not add payment URLs, account credentials, customer email allowlists, or secret keys to `index.html`: browser code is public, and local license flags are not secure access control. Before accepting payments, implement and test server-side checkout/entitlements, cancellation and refund handling, customer support, and truthful pricing disclosures; have the resulting flow and legal terms reviewed for the jurisdictions where you sell.
